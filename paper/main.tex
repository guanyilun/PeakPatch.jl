\begin{abstract}
We present \ppjl, a GPU-accelerated reimplementation of the mass-peak-patch
algorithm for generating full-sky halo catalogues and maps of CMB secondary
anisotropies. A multi-resolution decomposition of the initial Gaussian random
field --- a global coarse grid supplemented by independently synthesized fine
tiles with exact, tiling-independent noise --- removes the need for a global
fine-grid FFT, so that a $6144^3$-equivalent lightcone octant to $z=4.5$ runs
on a single multi-GPU node in hours. The pipeline produces abundance-matched
halo catalogues with second-order Lagrangian displacements and velocities, and
paints lensing convergence, kinetic and thermal Sunyaev--Zel'dovich, cosmic
infrared background, and integrated Sachs--Wolfe maps through a combination of
line-of-sight field kernels and halo-profile painting. We validate the pipeline
against the released Websky simulation products at both the catalogue level
(mass function, redshift distribution, clustering, halo bias, velocities, and
pairwise momentum, each at the few-per-cent level) and the map level
(angular power spectra of $\kappa$, kSZ, tSZ, CIB, and ISW maps within
$\sim$10--20\% of the released maps over the well-measured multipole ranges),
and we derive a coarse-grid resolution criterion for velocity-weighted
observables. \todo{performance numbers; data release statement}
\end{abstract}
