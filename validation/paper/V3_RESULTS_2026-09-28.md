⚠️ **AM caps the very top of the mass function.** AM N(>2e15, z<1) is 0 in every octant.
Tinker expects 1.56 per octant, 12.5 in total, and the *raw* catalogs have 11. The AM maps
the rank-1 halo of each Δz=0.1 bin to M(N_Tinker=1), about 1.7e15, so it cannot produce the
Poisson tail above that. This is a property of the AM scheme; it is **not yet fixed** and
needs a decision. Candidate fixes:
- abundance-match in cumulative z (one ranking over z<1 for the top N);
- keep raw masses above M(N≈few);
- sample the top ranks.

It affects only the ~10 most massive clusters in the sky.

## Update: the AM top cap, full-sky AM + tail_N (user-approved 2026-09-28; campaign "v3fs")

**Reference procedure.** Stein+2020 §4.1 and `~/work/peakpatch/python/catalogue_tools/abundance_match/`
use the same 10⁴ × 46 table method as ours, with two differences:
- It builds **one full-sky table**: all octants together, full-shell volume.
- Above a slice's top halo it leaves the table at **identity**. The bilinear lookup then
  blends the top halos with their raw masses. That is our "top-halo bug", fixed 2026-09-25
  by capping; the reference behaviour lets extreme halos partly keep raw masses and can
  invert ranks.

**Implemented** (`src/AbundanceMatch/AbundanceMatch.jl`):
- `am_grid` and `am_counts!` accumulate counts across catalogs and observers.
- `build_abundance_table(N_pp::Matrix, …)` builds the table from summed counts.
- `tail_N` holds the ratio M_target/M_raw constant above the last mass edge with ≥ tail_N
  halos per z-bin. This is monotone, so ranks are preserved.
- New tests are in `test_finalize_am.jl`; the CPU suite passes 1791/1791.

Driver: `validation/websky_6144/apply_abundance_match_fullsky.jl`.
